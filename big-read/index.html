Christian antichrists

Noun, plural

Those who use power to harm others in the name of Jesus.

When the word antichrist appears in the Bible, it is usually plural — not the single figure who populated the nightmares of fundamentalism's young people, arriving from somewhere outside. Antichrists originate within the church. They justify harm and vengeance theologically, pointing people away from the God who entered humanity as a colonized peasant in the hinterlands of the Roman empire.

The Book

Where did it
all go wrong?

“Quite simply, Defying Tyrants is a book every Christian should read.”

Kristin Kobes Du Mez, author of Jesus and John Wayne

Christian fixation with political power is the theological emergency of our time. Jesus directed his followers away from the race for power. Somewhere along the way, many Christians turned him into a mascot for imperial domination.

In Defying Tyrants, Matthew D. Taylor traces the origins of Christian supremacy in a trenchant reckoning with the murder, mayhem, and monsters of Christianity: the early church and the Crusades, colonialism, slavery, and the Holocaust. With cogent argument and moral precision, he excavates the scriptural texts and shows how imperialism infected the body of Christ.

It is not only an indictment. The book is also a search party. Taylor spotlights a countertradition of Christians who lived generous lives of sacrifice and stood up to authoritarians across the centuries, and he calls readers to find in the teachings of Jesus and the witness of the early church a model of rebellion against tyrants of all kinds.

Because if Jesus is Lord, Caesar isn't.

The Author

Matthew D. Taylor

Scholar of Christian Extremism

Matthew D. Taylor is a scholar of Christian extremism and the author of The Violent Take It by Force, a study of the movement that carried charismatic Christian networks into the centre of American politics. In Defying Tyrants he turns from one movement to the long history that made it possible.

Free to be Faithful readers have met him before: he taught The Christian–Jewish Question Today in spring 2026, and he returns in spring 2027 with Christian Antichrists, a six-week seminar built on this book.
