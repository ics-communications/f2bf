Spirals of Love and Healing

Empathy, Re-stor(y)-ing, Re-connecting, Transforming

 

Jim Olthuis · September 15 to October 20, 2026

This course explores the journey of personal healing as an interactive, inter-relational dance of trust and attunement. The therapeutic dance is a four-stage spiralling process: Letting-in (developing trust and safety), Letting-go (owning and visiting trauma and pain), Letting-out (releasing, rebirthing), and Letting-transform (re-integrating, re-connecting). In the process a sacred healing space of love opens up with its own unique rhythm and energy where love engages, heals, and transforms.

Participants will explore:

 
• Trace the four-stage spiral of letting-in, letting-go, letting-out, and letting-transform
• Explore healing as an inter-relational dance of trust and attunement
• Consider how a sacred space of love engages, heals, and transforms

Instructor: Jim Olthuis is professor emeritus of philosophical theology at ICS, where he taught for more than four decades, and a practising psychotherapist. His books include The Beautiful Risk and Dancing in the Wild Spaces of Love.

Keeping Faith Under Dictators

Lessons from 20th Century Europe

 

Bruce Berglund · September 16 to October 21, 2026

It’s nothing new — church members giving their support to authoritarian leaders, nationalist movements, and attacks against minority groups. The same pattern we see today runs through the history of 20th-century Europe. Why were so many Christians drawn to nationalism, racism, and tyranny, and how did other Christians express their opposition? This course explores the dynamics of resistance and complicity in these historical episodes, looking at the responses of ministers, artists, and intellectuals.

Participants will explore:

 
• Examine collaboration under fascist, Nazi, and communist regimes
• Read the texts of well-known opposition figures in 20th-century Europe, including Dietrich Bonhoeffer and Václav Havel
• Ask what faith under oppressive states teaches us about faith, the church, and morality

Instructor: Bruce Berglund is a historian of modern Central and Eastern Europe and the author of Castle and Cathedral in Modern Prague. He lives and teaches in Prague, and returns to Free to be Faithful after teaching Twentieth Century Authoritarianism.

“Broken Hallelujah”

Faith, Deconstruction, and Spirituality through Music

 

Angela Reitsma Bick · November 3 to December 8, 2026

What happens to faith when trust is broken, certainty gives way to doubt, or the Church no longer feels like home? Drawing on stories gathered for Blessed Are the Undone, this course explores experiences of faith, deconstruction, and spiritual longing through the music and poetry that give voice to what can be difficult to articulate. From Leonard Cohen’s “broken Hallelujah” to contemporary songwriters and poets, the course invites us to listen for the sacred beyond familiar religious forms.

Participants will explore:

 
• Listen to musicians and poets who wrestle with beauty and brokenness, doubt and belonging
• Engage stories of those dismissed or silenced within Christian communities
• Consider how art opens new possibilities for faith, meaning, and transformation

Instructor: Angela Reitsma Bick is the former editor-in-chief of Christian Courier and the author of Blessed Are the Undone, the book behind this course. She previously taught Reading the Ruins for Free to be Faithful.

“We Are Free to Change the World”

Hannah Arendt on Resistance, Hope, and the Power of the Possible

 

Ron Kuipers · November 5 to December 10, 2026

In our era of post-truth politics, scapegoating persecution of minorities, and re-emerging fascism, Hannah Arendt’s investigations of 20th century totalitarianism and the possibility of resisting its evitable rise have gained fresh urgency. This course introduces Arendt’s thought to new readers through Lyndsey Stonebridge’s acclaimed intellectual biography, We Are Free to Change the World: Hannah Arendt’s Lessons in Love and Disobedience (2024). Faced with what can feel like impenetrable darkness, Arendt insists that we remain free to resist evil’s suffocating allure and choose instead to love our broken, suffering world.

Participants will explore:

 
• Read Lyndsey Stonebridge’s acclaimed biography as an entry into Arendt’s thought
• Explore Arendt on love, natality, and the act of thinking itself
• Ask what her lessons hold for Christians seeking to offer shalom to a suffering world

Instructor: Ronald A. Kuipers is professor of philosophy of religion at ICS and director of the ICS Centre for Philosophy, Religion, and Social Ethics. His books include Richard Rorty, Critical Faith, and Solidarity and the Stranger. He teaches a recurring ICS graduate seminar on Arendt’s thought.

2026–27 Season

Later This Year

The rest of the year is set. Registration for each course opens closer to its start date, and full details are added as they are confirmed.

Winter 2027 · January to February

Readings for the Soul: More Encounters with the Good, the True, and the Beautiful

Karen Swallow Prior · Thursdays at 6:30 PM ET, January 7 to February 11, 2027

Through classic and contemporary fiction, drama, and poetry, we explore how literature awakens the moral imagination, deepens empathy, and helps us attend to goodness, truth, and beauty in unexpected places. The earlier course is not a prerequisite: returning and new participants are equally welcome.

Decolonizing The Great Commission

Joash Thomas · Tuesdays at 6:30 PM ET, January 12 to February 16, 2027

What if the way we have been taught to understand “The Great Commission” has more to do with colonial Christianity than with Jesus? Drawing on Fr. Joash P. Thomas’ Decolonizing the Great Commission series, this course reconsiders mission, evangelism, and discipleship through a decolonized, Jesus-shaped lens.
 

Spring 2027 · March to April

Christian Antichrists: Recovering a Biblical Category for a Christian Crisis

Matthew D. Taylor · Thursdays at 6:30 PM ET, March 4 to April 8, 2027

“Antichrist” has been misused for centuries as a weapon against outsiders. But the New Testament writers aimed the word inward, at those inside the community who bore Christ’s name while betraying his way. This course recovers that original usage and puts it to work as a diagnostic tool for the authoritarian Christianity of our own moment.
 

Summer 2027 · June to July

The Prophetic Imagination in Popular Culture

Mary McCampbell · Tuesdays at 6:30 PM ET, June 1 to July 6, 2027

We will explore popular literature, music, television, and film that grapple with complex theological questions. Borrowing Walter Brueggemann’s understanding of the “prophetic imagination,” we look at popular art that critiques the seductive, false notions of “the good life” that often lead us away from reality.

“Do Not Let Your Hearts Be Troubled”: Perspectives on Peace

Ethan van der Leek · Thursdays at 6:30 PM ET, June 3 to July 8, 2027

What does it mean to speak of peace in a world marked by war, political division, fractured communities, and inner turmoil? Drawing on biblical texts and voices from theology, philosophy, and activism, this course explores how different traditions have understood true peace, and develops tools for recognizing forms of false peace built on exclusion, suppressed difference, or coercion.

How It Works


Every Free to be Faithful course combines expert lectures with lively in-class discussion, delivered online via Zoom. Each course runs for six consecutive weeks, meeting once a week at 6:30 PM ET for two hours, with about 25 pages of accessible reading per week. Take a course as a non-credit participant for your own enrichment, take it for credit toward a Master of Worldview Studies degree, or simply learn alongside an enthusiastic community from across North America.


Tuition: $400 CAD non-credit · $1,500 CAD for credit. Scholarship funding is available; we don’t want tuition to stand in the way of anyone who would like to participate.


We hope you will consider joining us this year, and perhaps inviting a friend to learn alongside you.
