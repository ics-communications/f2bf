Spirals of Love and Healing: Empathy, Re-stor(y)-ing, Re-connecting, Transforming

Jim Olthuis · September 15 to October 20, 2026

This course will explore the journey of personal healing as an interactive, inter-relational dance of trust and attunement. The therapeutic dance is a four-stage spiralling process: Letting-in (developing trust and safety), Letting-go (owning and visiting trauma and pain), Letting-out (releasing, rebirthing), and Letting-transform (re-integrating, re-connecting). In the process a sacred healing space of love opens up with its own unique rhythm and energy where love engages, heals and transforms.

  • Trace the four-stage spiral of letting-in, letting-go, letting-out, and letting-transform
  • Explore healing as an inter-relational dance of trust and attunement
  • Consider how a sacred space of love engages, heals, and transforms

About the Instructor

Jim Olthuis is professor emeritus of philosophical theology at the Institute for Christian Studies, where he taught for more than four decades, and a practising psychotherapist. His books include The Beautiful Risk: A New Psychology of Loving and Being Loved and Dancing in the Wild Spaces of Love: A Theopoetics of Gift and Call, and he is the editor of Knowing Other-wise: Philosophy at the Threshold of Spirituality.

Keeping Faith Under Dictators: Lessons from 20th Century Europe

Bruce Berglund · September 16 to October 21, 2026

As we face the challenges of Christian nationalism and authoritarianism, we must remember that communities of faith have faced similar threats in the recent past. What lessons can we gain from European Christians of the last century, both Protestant and Catholic, who lived under fascist, Nazi, and communist regimes? This course explores the dynamics of resistance and complicity in these historical episodes as well as the theological responses of ministers, artists, and intellectuals. We will examine what compelled some Catholics and Protestants to embrace authoritarian politics while others resisted corruption of their faith. By investigating their search for certainty, solace, and wisdom, we will learn how Christians living in oppressive states can gain new understandings of God, the church, morality, and faith.

  • Examine resistance and complicity under fascist, Nazi, and communist regimes
  • Read the theological responses of ministers, artists, and intellectuals
  • Ask what faith under oppressive states teaches us about God, the church, and morality

About the Instructor

Bruce Berglund is a historian of modern Europe at Gustavus Adolphus College and the author of Castle and Cathedral in Modern Prague: Longing for the Sacred in a Skeptical Age. He returns to Free to be Faithful after teaching Twentieth Century Authoritarianism and joining the Big Read as a guest.

“Broken Hallelujah”: Faith, Deconstruction, and Spirituality through Music

Angela Reitsma Bick · November 3 to December 8, 2026

What happens to faith when trust is broken, certainty gives way to doubt, or the Church no longer feels like home? Drawing on stories gathered for Blessed Are the Undone, this course explores experiences of faith, deconstruction, and spiritual longing through the music and poetry that give voice to what can be difficult to articulate. Alongside stories of those who have felt dismissed or silenced within Christian communities, participants will engage contemporary musicians and poets whose work wrestles honestly with beauty and brokenness, doubt and belonging, loss and hope. From Leonard Cohen’s “broken Hallelujah” to the work of contemporary songwriters and poets, the course invites us to listen for the sacred beyond familiar religious forms, and to consider how art can open new possibilities for faith, meaning, and transformation.

  • Listen to musicians and poets who wrestle with beauty and brokenness, doubt and belonging
  • Engage stories of those dismissed or silenced within Christian communities
  • Consider how art opens new possibilities for faith, meaning, and transformation

About the Instructor

Angela Reitsma Bick is the former editor-in-chief of Christian Courier and the author of Blessed Are the Undone: Testimonies of the Quiet Deconstruction of Faith in Canada, the book behind this course. She previously taught Reading the Ruins for Free to be Faithful.

Spring 2027 · March to April

Christian Antichrists: Recovering a Biblical Category for a Christian Crisis

Matthew D. Taylor

“Antichrist” has been misused for centuries as a weapon against outsiders. But the New Testament writers aimed the word inward, at those inside the community who bore Christ’s name while betraying his way. This course recovers that original usage and puts it to work as a diagnostic tool for the authoritarian Christianity of our own moment.

Thursdays March 4 to April 8, 2027

Summer 2027 · June to July

The Prophetic Imagination in Popular Culture

Mary McCampbell

We will explore popular literature, music, television, and film that grapple with complex theological questions. Borrowing Walter Brueggemann’s understanding of the “prophetic imagination,” we look at popular art that critiques the seductive, false notions of “the good life” that often lead us away from reality.

Tuesdays June 1 to July 6, 2027

“Do Not Let Your Hearts Be Troubled”: Perspectives on Peace

Ethan van der Leek

What does it mean to speak of peace in a world marked by war, political division, fractured communities, and inner turmoil? Drawing on biblical texts and voices from theology, philosophy, and activism, this course explores how different traditions have understood true peace, and develops tools for recognizing forms of false peace built on exclusion, suppressed difference, or coercion.

How It Works

Learn at
Your Pace

Free to be Faithful courses involve a dynamic blend of expert lectures and in-class discussions, all delivered online via Zoom. Each course runs for six consecutive weeks, meeting once a week for two hours.

Take individual courses as a non-credit participant for your own interest and enrichment. Take them for credit as you can fit them into your schedule. Or complete the program and earn a Master of Worldview Studies (MWS) degree.

  • Weekly readings (~25 pages/week), accessible for a non-specialist audience
  • Two-hour Zoom seminars once a week for 6 consecutive weeks
  • Engagement with expert instructors and enthusiastic participants from across North America
  • Earn ICS credit toward a Master's degree, or simply deepen your faith
