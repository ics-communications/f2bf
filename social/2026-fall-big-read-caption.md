## Primary caption

When the word *antichrist* appears in the Bible, it is almost always plural.

Not the single figure who haunted fundamentalism's young people, arriving from somewhere outside. The New Testament aims the word inward — at people inside the community who bore Christ's name while betraying his way.

That is where Matthew D. Taylor starts in *Defying Tyrants*, and where he takes us next is the long, uncomfortable history: the early church and the Crusades, colonialism, slavery, the Holocaust. Christian fixation with political power, he argues, is the theological emergency of our time.

It is not only an indictment. The book is also a search party — for the Christians who lived generous lives of sacrifice and stood up to authoritarians anyway.

## Posting notes

- **Lead with the definition, not the title.** "Christian antichrists: those who use power to
  harm others in the name of Jesus" is the sharpest thing in the book and stops a scroll on its
  own. The title does not.
- **The endorsement is already on the cover** ("Courageous, clear-eyed, and utterly compelling"
  — Kristin Kobes Du Mez), so the caption does not need to repeat it. If you want Du Mez in the
  text, use the longer blurb instead: *"Quite simply, Defying Tyrants is a book every Christian
  should read."*
- **Free is the hook; say it early.** The two previous Big Reads drew their audience on the
  combination of a named author and no ticket price.
- **The excerpt is the conversion lever.** People who will not buy a book two days before an
  event will still trade an email address for a chapter. Keep it in every caption.
- **Do not promise a second speaker.** The conversation partner is not confirmed; both previous
  Big Reads paired the author with Kristin Kobes Du Mez, so expect to be asked. If you can
  confirm someone, the tile's byline block and the landing page's hero both have room.
- **Re-post after the stream** with the YouTube recording link, the way the July and September
  2025 Big Reads were handled.
